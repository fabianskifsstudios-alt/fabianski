Visual poetry · 001

Not the Same Oceans

An original poem by Fabianski

The fish was caught on a hook,
what once had been a decoy.
A stick, a need—the simplest need to eat.
She must catch fish to feed herself
and nurse her children.

Then cultures rose,
and man built power.
Yet before that, nature's power
had shaped the man:
the will to sail,
the power to feed nations.
From rations meant to keep us alive
grew the need to belong, to endure.
Yet there is no peace;
war claims its place in human nature.

The lonely child caught fish on an island,
barehanded, along a distant, deserted shore.
He grew to become a king,
eating raw fish,
killing only what he needed and nothing more.

Then came the evolutionary gift
of self-awareness.
His mind began to hunger
at a terrible cost.
No raw fish could satisfy his thirst
for more blood, more power.
No heart was needed
once a man's soul was lost.

Once, the fish was clean.
Now, entire species face extinction.
Man possessed the knowledge to preserve:
take only what is needed,
give back, plant again,
release the fish
that will not fit within your hand.

But man keeps starving,
starving until he grows mad.
He never gives back.
The waters grow barren,
the world grows contaminated.
Then he tries to eat the raw fish again—
the very source of his power.

But it is not the same fish.
It is no longer pure.
Darkness lives within it.
The man's soul, stained with blood,
has poisoned what once sustained him.

The ocean is no longer the same.
It mirrors the soul of modern man,
fed by gluttony,
corrupted by sin.

Nature's power
had shaped the man.

Nature's power
can clean the fish, again.

Architect of Silence

An original poem by Fabianski

We learn to be silent
before we learn why.

A child studies the room,
the weight of a voice,
the distance between footsteps,
the weather inside another person's eyes.

And slowly,
without anyone asking,
he becomes an architect.

He builds walls
where there should have been windows.
He seals the doors
that once opened toward the light.

He calls it strength.

Years pass.

The world applauds the man
who never breaks,
who carries his burdens without sound,
who answers I'm fine
as though it were a sacred oath.

But no one asks
what it costs to remain standing
when every room inside him
has been abandoned.

He grows older
and mistakes distance for peace.

He learns to leave
before he can be left.
To forget
before memory can wound him.
To turn tenderness into humor,
fear into anger,
loneliness into work.

And the architecture grows.

One day,
he hears laughter
coming from a room
he cannot remember building.

He stands before the door.

On the other side
is the person he might have been
if he had ever felt safe enough
to remain unfinished.

He reaches for the handle.

It does not turn.

For the first time,
he understands:

A wall can protect a man
from the world.

It can also protect the world
from ever knowing him.

And perhaps healing
is not the courage
to build something stronger.

Perhaps it is the courage
to let the light in
and begin dismantling
what once kept us alive.

For he also knows
the tools to break the walls.

And in this moment,
we offer hope
to the lonely architect.

The hope to learn
how to speak up,
to find the voice
buried beneath the silence,
and ultimately,
to make enough noise
to be heard.

About Fabianski

Fabianski is an independent creative project exploring the human condition through poetry, music, and moving images.

Each work is an invitation to look beneath the surface—to question what we inherit, what we destroy, and what remains.
